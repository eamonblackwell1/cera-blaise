The decision

Two ways to do this.

Both routes start in the north and end in Tokyo, so you move in one direction and never double back. Both put the dragon's lake on May 3, the one stretch when Tokyo is best avoided anyway (Golden Week holidays run May 3 to 5).

The difference is where the ceremony happens. Route A holds it in Kyoto and uses Fuji for your portraits. Route B holds it at Fuji. We recommend Route A, and the reasons are under each route. Read both, tell us which one is yours, and we start building it.

Route A · Recommended

Ceremony in Kyoto, portraits at Fuji

The wedding at Yoshida Shrine, a 1,000-year-old shrine on a wooded hill in Kyoto, with a team we have run this ceremony with before. Fuji comes two days later, in kimono, with two mornings of chances for the photo and nothing riding on the weather. One line south, no backtracking. May 10 is a Monday, inside your window.

Route B · Alternative

Ceremony at Fuji

The wedding at Kitaguchi Hongu Fuji Sengen Shrine at the foot of the mountain, on Thursday May 6. The picture you first described, held in one place on one day of mountain weather. Early May visibility is close to a coin flip, the route passes through Tokyo twice, and it would be our first ceremony with this shrine.

A question for you

How much of Tokyo do you want?

On both routes, Tokyo is the tattoo day plus MagicCon. There is almost no free Tokyo time for the three of you. That is a choice, not an accident: your list is almost entirely outside the city, and we built the trip around it.

If you want a slower Tokyo (Jackson at TeamLab, Akihabara, Nakano Broadway for Blaise, a day of nothing), the simplest trades are these:

  • Drop the Hachimantai spare day and arrive in Tokyo a day earlier.
  • Fold the Seki forge into the Gujo day and give the saved day to Tokyo.
  • In Route A, do Fuji as a long day trip instead of a ryokan night, which frees a Tokyo night.

Tell us on the call which way you lean and we cut the next version to match.

The ceremony

Where you marry.

Our recommendationYoshida Shrine, Kyoto

A thousand-year-old shrine on a wooded hill

Yoshida Shrine was founded in 859 on Mt. Yoshida beside Kyoto University. Quiet, old, almost no tourists. The ceremony runs about 30 minutes: purification, the priest's prayer, the ring exchange, san-san-kudo with a non-alcoholic cup, the tamagushi offering. Everyone attends. Guests sit inside the hall with you.

Why this works

  • You asked for a real Shinto ceremony, not a performance of one. This is a working shrine with its own priests, and the ritual is the same one Japanese couples have here.
  • You asked for a red torii. There are torii on site for the portraits straight after.
  • Dinner follows at a ryotei, a traditional restaurant with private tatami rooms, with a kaiseki course and a non-alcoholic pairing.
  • Our Kyoto attire team handles dressing, hair and makeup, the photographer and the shrine arrangements. We have run this format twice already.

Nineteen hundred years of history, part of the Mt. Fuji World Heritage listing, and the traditional start of the Yoshida climbing trail. Your list

Every item, answered.

  • Dragon lake in northern Japan Yes

    The Hachimantai Dragon Eye, a crater pond at 1,600 m whose spring snowmelt forms a ring like a dragon's eye. It opens in stages through May. In your window you will see the snowfield, the blue crescent forming at the edge, and the 27 km snow corridor road with walls up to 8 m. The full ring usually comes later in May, and we say this now so there are no surprises. We pair it with Lake Tazawa, 40 minutes away, Japan's deepest lake and home of the Tatsuko dragon legend. Taking lake water home is fine. It goes in a sealed bottle in checked luggage.

  • The water city, Gujo-Hachiman Yes

    Canals with koi running past the houses, the Sogi spring, the castle above the town. Hands-on: plastic food sample making (Gujo invented it) and a local indigo or textile workshop we are confirming. Two nights in a town ryokan.

  • Ninjutsu class for Blaise Yes

    Iga, the historic ninja homeland, a day trip from Kyoto. A program designed by the Japan Ninja Council on the site of a mountain castle used for real ninja training: breathing, hand signs and walking techniques in the dojo, then the forest for sword, grappling hook and concealment. We are also tracing where your Facebook video was filmed and will match it if we can.

  • Tattoo in Tokyo Yes

    May 13, after every onsen on the trip. Three Tides is a solid choice. We are shortlisting two or three artists whose dragons match your sketch-style references, including one who works in color for the jade option. The deposit and design brief go in early, since the good artists book months out.

  • Tattoo-friendly onsen Yes

    Three chances before the tattoo: the Tazawa ryokan, the Gujo ryokan, and the Fuji ryokan with private open-air baths in the room, which makes the tattoo question irrelevant for the whole group.

  • Indigo dyeing Yes

    Gujo and the Hida region both have working indigo dyers. We are confirming a workshop where you dye your own piece on May 6.

  • Making or buying a sword Yes

    Seki, 30 minutes from Gujo, has made blades for 700 years. Two options: a private forging demonstration beside a working forge with English narration, or a full day where you forge a small blade or kitchen knife with a licensed swordsmith and take it home. Buying a real sword to export involves paperwork we will walk you through.

  • Coin ring Swapped

    Your instinct was right. It is the touristy one, and reshaping Japanese coins sits in a legal grey area. The forged knife in Seki is the better "make something and take it home".

  • Traditional Japanese candles Yes

    Near Okazaki Castle on the Ghibli Park route: watch the craftsmen make warosoku (plant-wax candles) the Edo way, then paint your own. The 240-year-old Mishima shop in Hida-Furukawa is the purist's option if you want the detour.

  • Fushimi Inari Yes

    Dawn on May 9. The gates are nearly empty before 7, and the walk up through the thousands of torii is the best hour of Kyoto.

  • Koi history museum Cut, for now

    It exists (Nishikigoi no Sato in Ojiya, Niigata) but it is a full day off every route. Gujo's canals give you the koi. If it matters more than we think, say so and we will price the detour.

  • Power Rangers museum Answered differently

    There is no permanent museum. Super Sentai, the original of Power Rangers, ended its 50-year run in February 2026. What exists: Toei Kyoto Studio Park, the studio that made it, with a permanent Super Sentai History exhibit, a location gallery and a live hero show (May 9). And a touring All Super Sentai Exhibition, 500 real props, suits and robots, which moves city to city. We are asking the organizer where it will be in May 2027. If it is anywhere on the route, the itinerary bends to it. Blaise may already know: Mighty Morphin was adapted from Kyoryu Sentai Zyuranger (1992).

  • Studio Ghibli, a full day Upgraded

    The Mitaka museum is a 2 to 3 hour visit and tickets are a lottery. Ghibli Park in Aichi is the full day you asked for, with Satsuki and Mei's house, the Valley of Witches and Mononoke Village. It sits on the route between Gujo and Kyoto. Tickets release two months ahead and we book them the hour they open.
